Option A: nested toggles under each zone

Each flood zone has its own checkbox. Underneath it, an indented "Include climate change" checkbox adds that zone's climate change extent.

Option B | Option C | Overview, styling and open questions

How it works

  • The "Climate change" radios (Present day or 2070 to 2125) are replaced by checkboxes under "Flood zones", so one zone can show climate change while the other does not.
  • Climate change checkboxes are greyed out and switched off when their parent zone is unchecked.
  • The key is grouped by zone: each zone lists its present day and climate change extent together.
{% include "design-spec/fz-climate-change-layers-v3-10-0-0/partials/embed.html" %}

What works well

  • Makes the link between a zone and its climate change extent obvious. The question "what does zone 3 look like with climate change?" is answered in one place.
  • Fewer top level choices. Four layers become two zones, each with an extra option.
  • The key mirrors the menu, so users can match the two.

Risks and things to test

  • Removes the "Climate change" radios that users know today. Users who expect to switch the whole map to "2070 to 2125" now need two clicks.
  • Hiding a zone also hides its climate change extent. Users might want to see climate change without the present day layer, which this option does not allow.
  • Nested checkboxes are harder to use on mobile. Check touch target size (at least 44 by 44 pixels, so more than the GOV.UK small checkbox).
  • Biggest change to the map code: the menu structure and the key both change.

Accessibility notes

  • Group the checkboxes in a fieldset with the legend "Flood zones". Label the nested checkboxes in full for screen reader users, for example "Flood zone 2: include climate change (2070 to 2125)".
  • When a parent is unchecked, disable the nested checkbox and keep its state, so it comes back on when the parent is re-checked.
  • Colour is not the only cue. The two climate change colours differ in lightness, every key item has a text label, and the info panel names the layer in words (see the styling rules).
